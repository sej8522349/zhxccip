What is Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo and how is it diagnosed?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is one of the most common causes of dizziness and vertigo, occurring when tiny calcium crystals in the inner ear become dislodged and shift with head movement. Symptoms are typically triggered by changes in head position, such as rolling over in bed, looking up, or bending down. Doctors diagnose BPPV using specific physical tests, like the Dix-Hallpike maneuver, which check for nystagmus—an involuntary eye movement that signals inner ear disturbance.

Dizziness can stem from many causes beyond BPPV, including migraines, low blood pressure, or neurological conditions. Because the right treatment depends on the exact cause, understanding your symptoms early is key.
