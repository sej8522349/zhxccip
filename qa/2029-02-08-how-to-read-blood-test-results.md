# How to Read Blood Test Results

How to Read Blood Test Results

When it comes to your health and wellness, there are a variety of tests that doctors perform to determine your health status. A blood test also referred to as a blood panel or a complete blood count (CBC) test, is valuable for checking many things. This includes the health of your vital organs, such as the heart, and the presence of dangerous genetic conditions.

Additionally, a CBC test is one of the best ways to determine your overall health and wellness. A good result on your blood test means that you’re in good physical condition, but anomalies could indicate problems. However, CBC tests are fairly complicated to understand, and a misinterpretation could be just as dangerous as a bad result on the blood test.

Therefore, if you’re considering taking a blood test and want to know how to interpret the results, you’ve come to the right place. While your doctor will most likely explain the results, it’s good to have a basic understanding of blood tests going into your examination. This article will also discuss preparing for your blood test so you don’t receive inconclusive results.

Understanding Blood Test Results

Complete blood count tests, the most common type of blood test, are confusing and tricky. They measure many different aspects of your blood, including your white blood cell count, red blood cell count, platelets, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and more. Even though you’ll get a full explanation from the doctor that administers the test, having a previous understanding of blood tests will help you fully understand the results.

Reference Ranges

The first thing you need to understand about blood tests is that they’re based on reference ranges. Reference ranges refer to the normal range for most people who get tested. For instance, if the reference range for a specific section of the test is 70 – 90, your result should fall within that range.

However, it’s important to understand that everyone’s bodies are different, and just because you didn’t score within the “normal range” doesn’t mean you have a problem. Therefore, reference ranges are typically used as a rough guide rather than the rule of law when determining your health status.

Abnormal Results

The second thing you need to understand about blood tests is what it means when you receive abnormal results. An abnormal result simply means that your test results were slightly higher or lower than the “normal” reference range.

While this may seem concerning, it isn’t always something to worry about. As with blood pressure and heart rate, everyone’s body is different, which means that a perfectly healthy person can test outside of the normal reference range. It’s also possible for someone who’s unhealthy to test within the normal reference range. These types of tests are known as false positives or false negatives.

False positives

A false positive means that your blood test reveals a disease or condition you don’t have.

False negatives

A false negative means that your blood test reveals you don’t have any diseases or conditions when you do.

Because of the dangers of false positives and negatives, you should have your test interpreted by a medical professional. They will know whether or not you need additional tests.

Tips for Preparing for a Blood Test

If you want to prevent an inconclusive reading or abnormal result on your blood test, here are a few tips to follow.

Fasting Requirements

One of the most common reasons for abnormal or inconclusive results on blood tests is that people didn’t follow the fasting requirements. It’s important to fast for a minimum of 8 to 12 hours before your blood test; some doctors will recommend up to 24 hours. You should also avoid drinking anything besides water while you’re fasting.

Medications and Supplements

In the same way that food and drink can result in abnormal or inconclusive results on your blood test, so can medications and supplements. However, this isn’t always the case, and you should check with the doctor providing the test to find out if any supplements or medications you’re taking could impede the results.

Conclusion

Receiving an abnormal result on your blood work can be concerning and inconvenient. Either something is potentially wrong with you, or your blood test got messed up, and you’ll need to take a second one. Therefore, following the tips and tricks above in the days leading up to your blood test is important.

Having your blood test administered, interpreted, and explained by a medical professional is also important. A professional can determine if abnormal results are cause for concern and if further testing is necessary. They will also ensure that the test is administered properly and that you know exactly what they mean.
