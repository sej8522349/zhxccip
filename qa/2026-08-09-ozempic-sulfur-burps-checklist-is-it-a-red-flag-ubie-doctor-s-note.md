What Are Sulfur Burps?

Sulfur burps (also called "rotten egg burps") are belches that smell strongly of hydrogen sulfide gas. Common causes include:

Dietary factors: High-protein, sulfur-rich foods (eggs, meat, cruciferous veggies)

Digestive changes: Slower stomach emptying, bacterial overgrowth

Medications: Drugs that affect gut motility or acid levels

In most cases, sulfur burps are more of an annoyance than a danger. But with Ozempic, they can indicate how your body is adapting—or, rarely, a complication requiring medical attention.

When to Be Cautious: Red Flags

Some signs suggest you should take sulfur burps more seriously. Seek medical advice if you experience:

Severe or worsening abdominal pain

Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down

Unexplained weight loss >5% of body weight

Signs of dehydration (dizziness, dark urine)

High fever, chills, or unexplained fatigue

Blood in vomit or stool

These symptoms could indicate complications such as:

Gastroparesis: Extreme slowing of stomach emptying leading to pain and malnutrition

Pancreatitis: Inflammation of the pancreas presenting with severe upper abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting

Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO): Excess bacteria producing gas and malabsorption

Peptic Ulcer Disease: Ulcers causing burning pain, bleeding, or anemia

Managing Burps Without Stopping Ozempic

Many people benefit from continuing Ozempic despite mild GI side effects. If your burps are manageable:

Ask your provider about dose titration: Slower dose increases can ease GI symptoms.

Consider probiotics: Certain strains may help balance gut bacteria.

Explore digestive enzymes: Over-the-counter forms can assist in breaking down sulfur-rich foods.

Always check with your doctor before adding supplements or making major dietary shifts.

When to Seek Emergency Care

Go to the nearest ER or call emergency services if you have:

Sudden, severe upper abdominal pain radiating to your back

Continuous vomiting with inability to keep liquids down

Signs of shock: very low blood pressure, confusion, rapid heartbeat

Vomiting or passing blood

These could signal life-threatening conditions like acute pancreatitis or gastrointestinal bleeding.
