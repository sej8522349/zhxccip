Risks of PRP Injections in the Scalp: PRP for Hair Loss Side Effects

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy has become a popular non-surgical option for hair restoration. By using your own blood's growth factors, practitioners inject PRP into the scalp to stimulate hair follicles. While many patients tolerate the procedure well, it's important to understand potential PRP for hair loss side effects before you decide.

What Is PRP Therapy for Hair Loss?

PRP involves:

Drawing a small amount of your blood.

Spinning it in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets.

Injecting the platelet-rich plasma into areas of thinning hair.

Growth factors in platelets can promote healing, improve blood flow, and potentially stimulate dormant hair follicles. Sessions are usually repeated every 4–6 weeks for the first few months, then maintenance treatments every 3–6 months.

Common, Mild Side Effects

Most side effects are mild and temporary. They include:

Pain or discomfort at injection sitesSlight burning or stinging during and after injections is normal. Applying ice or a topical numbing cream can help.

While rare, more significant complications can arise if proper technique or sterile practices aren't followed:

Scar tissue formationRepeated injections might lead to small areas of fibrosis (thickened tissue), which could affect hair growth if extensive.

Nerve injuryImproper injection depth or angle could irritate or damage small nerves, causing temporary numbness or tingling.

Blood vessel injuryAccidental injection into a blood vessel can cause bruising or, very rarely, vascular complications.

Who Should Use Caution or Avoid PRP?

Before undergoing PRP therapy, discuss your full medical history with a qualified provider. PRP may not be suitable if you have:

Platelet disorders or low platelet count

Active infections or skin conditions on the scalp (e.g., eczema, psoriasis)

Blood clotting disorders or are taking blood thinners

Cancer or are undergoing chemotherapy

Severe anemia or other blood diseases

Certain autoimmune conditions

Pregnancy and breastfeeding aren't well studied—let your provider know if you're pregnant, planning pregnancy, or nursing.

Minimizing Your Risks

To reduce the likelihood of PRP for hair loss side effects:

Choose a credentialed dermatologist, plastic surgeon, or experienced medical professional.

Confirm the clinic follows strict sterile technique.

Ask about the centrifuge protocol and platelet concentration—standardized methods produce more predictable results.

Ensure topical anesthetics and additives are safe for you.

Follow pre- and post-procedure instructions carefully (e.g., avoid NSAIDs for a few days before treatment if advised).

Recognizing When to Seek Help

After PRP treatment, contact your provider or seek medical attention if you experience:

Fever, chills, or flu-like symptoms

Severe or worsening pain

Red streaks or spreading redness on the scalp

Excessive swelling, bruising, or bleeding
