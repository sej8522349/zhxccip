Why Can't You Conceive? PRP for Infertility: Medically Approved Next Steps

Struggling to conceive can feel overwhelming, especially when you've tried multiple approaches without success. Infertility affects roughly 15% of couples worldwide, with causes ranging from hormonal imbalances to lifestyle factors. Recently, Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy has emerged as a promising option. Below, we explore common infertility reasons, explain how PRP for infertility works, review the evidence, and outline the next medically approved steps you can take.

Common Reasons You Might Be Unable to Conceive

Infertility can stem from one or both partners. Understanding potential causes is the first step toward targeted treatment.

Female factors

Ovulatory disorders (e.g., polycystic ovary syndrome, thyroid issues)

Tubal blockages or scarring (often from infections or endometriosis)

Uterine abnormalities (fibroids, polyps, thin endometrial lining)

Diminished ovarian reserve (fewer or lower-quality eggs)

Male factors

Low sperm count or poor sperm motility

Structural issues (varicocele)

Hormonal imbalances

Genetic or chromosomal anomalies

Unexplained infertility

After standard testing, about 10–20% of couples receive no clear diagnosis

Lifestyle factors (stress, weight, smoking, environmental toxins) may play a role

What Is PRP Therapy?

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy uses a concentrated portion of your own blood to promote tissue healing. Here's how it applies to infertility:

Blood draw: A small sample of your blood is taken.

Centrifugation: The sample is spun to separate platelets from red blood cells.

Activation: Platelets are activated to release growth factors.

Injection: The PRP solution is injected into target areas (ovaries, endometrium, testes).

Key growth factors in PRP help stimulate cell regeneration, improve blood flow, and support healthy tissue function.

PRP for Infertility: What the Research Shows

PRP for infertility is still under study, but early results are encouraging:

Ovarian Rejuvenation• Small clinical trials report improved ovarian function in women with diminished ovarian reserve.• Some participants showed rises in anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), suggesting increased ovarian activity.

Endometrial Lining Enhancement• Thin lining (below 7 mm) can hinder embryo implantation.• PRP intrauterine infusion has been linked to thicker endometria and higher implantation rates in IVF cycles.

Male Factor Infertility• Limited studies indicate PRP injections into the testes may improve sperm count and motility.• More rigorous trials are needed, but initial safety data are positive.

While promising, PRP for infertility remains an off-label treatment in many regions. Always discuss with a fertility specialist before proceeding.

Who Might Benefit from PRP for Infertility?

Candidates often include:

Women over 35 with low ovarian reserve

Patients with repeated IVF implantation failures

Those diagnosed with thin endometrial lining

Men with idiopathic low sperm quality

Couples seeking minimally invasive adjunct therapies

What to Expect During PRP Treatment

If you and your doctor decide PRP for infertility is worth trying, here's a general outline:

Pre-treatment evaluation

Hormone levels (FSH, LH, AMH, estradiol)

Ultrasound to assess ovaries or endometrium

Semen analysis for male partners

Procedure day

Blood draw (30–60 mL)

PRP preparation (20–30 minutes)

Targeted injection under ultrasound guidance

Post-procedure

Mild discomfort or cramping for 24–48 hours

Avoid strenuous exercise for 48 hours

Monitor cycles (women) or repeat semen analysis (men) after 1–3 months

Most clinics repeat PRP injections over 2–3 cycles, monitoring responses before moving to IVF or other treatments.

Other Medically Approved Next Steps

No single approach suits everyone. Depending on your diagnosis, consider combining PRP with:

Lifestyle modifications• Balanced diet rich in antioxidants• Regular, moderate exercise• Stress-reduction techniques (yoga, meditation)• Smoking cessation and limiting alcohol

Hormonal therapies• Clomiphene citrate or letrozole for ovulation induction• Gonadotropin injections for controlled ovarian stimulation

Assisted reproductive technologies (ART)• Intrauterine insemination (IUI) for mild male or unexplained infertility• In vitro fertilization (IVF) with or without intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)

Surgical interventions• Hysteroscopic removal of fibroids or polyps• Laproscopic correction of endometriosis or tubal blockages
