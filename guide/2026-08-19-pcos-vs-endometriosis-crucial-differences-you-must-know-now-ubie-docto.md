What are the differences between Polycystic Ovary Syndrome and endometriosis?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) and endometriosis are two distinct gynecological conditions that affect women's reproductive health in very different ways. PCOS is a hormonal disorder characterized by elevated androgen levels, irregular menstrual cycles, and small fluid-filled sacs (cysts) on the ovaries. Endometriosis, on the other hand, occurs when tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus, often causing pelvic pain, heavy periods, and fertility challenges. While both conditions can impact fertility and quality of life, their causes, symptoms, and treatments differ significantly.

Because PCOS and endometriosis share overlapping symptoms like pelvic discomfort and menstrual irregularities, self-diagnosis can be misleading, and untreated conditions may worsen over time. Explanation

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) and endometriosis are two distinct conditions that affect women's reproductive health, but they have different causes and symptoms.

PCOS:

PCOS is a hormonal disorder where women may have irregular menstrual periods, excess hair growth, acne, and obesity.

The ovaries may develop numerous small collections of fluid (follicles) and fail to regularly release eggs.

Hormonal imbalances are a key feature, often involving elevated levels of male hormones.

Endometriosis:

Endometriosis occurs when tissue similar to the lining inside the uterus grows outside the uterus.

It can cause pain, especially during menstrual periods, and may lead to fertility problems.
