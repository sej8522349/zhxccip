What is the evidence supporting apple cider vinegar's role in lowering blood sugar levels?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Apple cider vinegar (ACV) may help lower blood sugar levels, according to several studies. Research suggests that consuming ACV before or with meals could improve insulin sensitivity and reduce post-meal blood glucose spikes, particularly in people with insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes. However, more research is needed to fully understand how it works, the ideal dosage, and its long-term effects on blood sugar management.

Apple cider vinegar (ACV) has been studied for its potential to lower blood sugar levels. Some research indicates that ACV might help improve blood sugar control. For example, one study found that ACV improved blood sugar levels in people with diabetes. Another study suggested that ACV could help with glycemic control, which means it might help keep blood sugar levels stable.

However, while these studies show promising results, more research is needed to fully understand how ACV works and its long-term effects. It is important to note that while ACV may help with blood sugar levels, it should not replace any treatments or medications prescribed by a healthcare provider.
