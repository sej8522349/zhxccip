Is high dose vitamin C a viable cancer treatment?

Studies linking high dose vitamin C and cancer treatment have been ongoing since the 1970s. This connection was made famous in 1976 by Linus Pauling, a double Nobel Prize winner. His studies showed increased longevity in terminally ill cancer patients who took daily doses of vitamin C, both orally and intravenously.

While Pauling’s results have not been duplicated, some evidence shows that vitamin C can slow down the multiplication of cancerous cells. It also reduces the side effects of chemotherapy, such as nausea, fatigue, and vomiting, therefore improving the patient’s quality of life.

How much vitamin C should I take daily?

The daily recommended dose of vitamin C for adults is about 90mg, with a higher limit of 2000mg per day. Because vitamin C is water-soluble, the body does not store any excess amounts but rather expels them in urine. When taken orally, doses over 1000mg can cause diarrhea and irritate the digestive tract.

Intravenous delivery, on the other hand, can allow for much higher doses as it bypasses the digestive system. This dose can be adjusted depending on your health condition and needs. Too much vitamin C is rarely lethal, but should be administered by qualified personnel.
