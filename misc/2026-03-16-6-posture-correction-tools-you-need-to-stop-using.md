# 6 Posture Correction Tools You Need to Stop Using

[Need to Stop](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/)

Athlean-X expert Jeff Cavaliere C.S.C.S. is all about showing you how to enhance your workouts—and that also includes what you should be doing when you're out of the gym. Sure, he'll lead you through biceps pushups and bodyweight exercises, but there's more to training your body correctly than just the exercises you're doing. Your posture is important too, whether you're mid-workout or mid-workday.

Cavaliere took some time to highlight the problems with several of the most common methods people use to adjust their posture—and what he wants you to do to make it better .

[to Stop Using](https://collagen-atelier.bbsxyxy.com/blog/2863667845.html)

"If you have rounded shoulders, a forward head or even a slumped posture due to a pelvic tilt, then you will want to be aware that reaching for the wrong devices to help fix these conditions is going to leave you with the same problems and with less money in your pocket," says Cavaliere. "Not everything promoted as a posture fix is actually going to do anything to fix your posture. If anything, it can actually make it worse."

Here are six tools and practices you should ditch if you're trying to improve your posture.

Sitting on a Physio Ball

"When used as a posture improver by sitting on it rather than a chair, you are actually inviting more instability and postural issues into your life," says Cavaliere. "The lack of back support that you get from switching to a ball over a chair is something that becomes more dangerous when the inevitable fatigue of the back muscles comes in that you’re trying to improve by ditching the seat."

This creates more instability from below.

"The pelvis can shift left and right, the pelvis can rock forward and back. You're lacking everything you need to maintain this," says Cavaliere.

Back/Posture Braces

"Guess what? They don't work," says Cavaliere. "If you're wearing a brace to put you in the right posture, is sort of like the equivalent of me putting Jessie in a full-Nelson to get his shoulders back. It's not going to do anything long-term."

Instead, he suggests doing actual back-strengthening exercises.

Posture Correctors

Cavaliere also takes umbrage with products that scold the wearer when they fall out of perfect posture, which often come in the form of a device that beeps or buzzes as soon as you slouch. The problem? There's no focus on the actual solutions.

[to Stop Using](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/)

"That's like a doctor telling you hey, you've gotta quick smoking," he says. "A reminder doesn't do anything—actually quitting smoking might actually get you to the point where you're getting healthier."

Yoga

Cavaliere also suggests getting into a yoga practice, not just using it as a quick way to warm up or as a quick fix to your issues.

"Yoga is a practice that needs to be given full attention, effort and dedication. If done this way, it can lead to amazing increases in flexibility, muscular endurance and mobility," says Cavaliere.

Standing Desks

"A lot of us have made the switch to go from our sitting desk to a standing desk, and somehow that's going to fix all of our problems. Actually, I think it's going to introduce a lot more problems than sitting down," says Cavaliere.

However, you can also have terrible posture as you stand, which can lead to issues.

"Crossing the feet and dropping the pelvis to one side, slumping forward, etc. All of these are things that can happen just as easily from your feet," says Cavaliere. "As a side note, most muscular pain that comes as a result of poor posture is going to be aggravated in the standing position and eased when sitting."

Foam Rollers

Cavaliere likens the use of foam rollers to his qualms with non-committed yogis. "If that's the approach you're going to take with the foam roller, then do me a favor and don't use it at all, because you can do more damage with this than benefit if you don't know what you're doing," he says. "The fact is how you roll, when you roll, what muscles you're rolling, the direction you're rolling in, and how long you're doing it all matters here."

Foam rolling, or self-myofascial release, is a practice used to break up trigger points of knots in your muscles, not a solution to force your spine and shoulders into place. Use your roller to smooth out your trouble spots—but don't expect that you'll automatically start standing up straighter afterwards.

Instead of these quick fixes, you need to invest your time in building up a stronger body to solve your posture problem. "Take the time to strengthen your back using the exercises that will help you to fix this long term," Cavaliere says, offering his posture correction video as guidance.
