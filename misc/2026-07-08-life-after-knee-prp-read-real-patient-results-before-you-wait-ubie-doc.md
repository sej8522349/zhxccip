What Is PRP Therapy?

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma. It's a concentration of your own blood platelets, growth factors, and plasma proteins that can stimulate healing. Here's how it typically works:

A small blood sample is drawn from your arm.

The sample is spun in a centrifuge to separate the platelets and plasma from red blood cells.

The concentrated PRP is injected into the painful area of the knee, guided by ultrasound for accuracy.

The goal is to jump-start the body's natural repair mechanisms, potentially reducing inflammation and encouraging tissue regeneration.

Potential Risks and Side Effects

PRP is generally safe because it uses your own blood, but it's not without risks. You should know:

Injection-site pain or soreness for 24–72 hours

Mild swelling or bruising

Risk of infection (rare)

No guarantee of complete pain relief

Possible need for repeat injections if benefits fade

Always discuss serious symptoms—like fever, severe swelling, or worsening pain—with your doctor. If you believe you have a serious complication, speak to a medical professional immediately.

Who Is a Good Candidate?

PRP isn't for everyone. Ideal candidates often:

Are under 70 with mild to moderate osteoarthritis

Have tried conservative measures (physical therapy, NSAIDs, lifestyle changes)

Want to delay or avoid knee replacement surgery

Are in good general health (no active infections, blood disorders, or severe cardiopulmonary disease)

People with advanced arthritis, significant cartilage loss, or certain blood disorders may see less benefit. A consultation with an orthopedic specialist can clarify whether PRP makes sense for your specific condition.

Preparing for Your PRP Injection

To get the most from your treatment:

Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) for at least 48 hours before your injection.

Stay hydrated and get a good night's sleep.

Wear loose, comfortable clothing to your appointment.

Arrange a ride if you anticipate discomfort driving afterwards.

Next Steps and Considerations

Before scheduling PRP therapy:

Get imaging (X-ray or MRI) to assess joint damage

Confirm you're medically clear for injections (no active infections or blood disorders)

Discuss realistic expectations: PRP can reduce pain and improve function but rarely restores a joint to "brand new"

PRP is not a substitute for urgent care or emergency treatment. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

Sudden, severe knee swelling with redness and warmth

Signs of infection (fever, chills, draining wound)

An inability to bear any weight on the knee

Numbness or tingling that spreads down the leg

Speak to a Doctor

While many patients achieve meaningful relief from PRP injections, every case is unique. Always discuss treatment options with a qualified healthcare professional, especially if you have other medical conditions or take blood-thinning medications. If you have life-threatening or serious symptoms, speak to a doctor or visit the emergency department right away.
