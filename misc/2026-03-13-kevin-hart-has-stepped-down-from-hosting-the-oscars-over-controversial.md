# Kevin Hart Has Stepped Down from Hosting the Oscars Over Controversial Tweets

Kevin Hart looked like the answer to the Oscars' prayers when news broke that he would host the 2019 awards. But nearly as quickly as that came to light, the comedian stepped down from hosting duties following backlash over some of his previous comments.

Days after he announced that he would be MC of the Oscars, criticism piled up online over years-old tweets in which Hart used the word "gay" as a slur or otherwise wrote in ways considered homophobic.

The 39-year-old comedian and movie star's response was initially defensive. “Guys, I’m almost 40 years old,” he said in an Instagram video. “If you don’t believe that people change, grow, evolve as they get older, I don’t know what to tell you. If you want to hold people in a position where they always have to justify or explain the past, then do you. I’m the wrong guy, man. I’m in a great place. A great mature place where all I do is spread positivity.”

He reversed that posture by Friday. “I have made the choice to step down from hosting this year’s Oscar’s,” he wrote on Twitter. “This is because I do not want to be a distraction on a night that should be celebrated by so many amazing talented artists. I sincerely apologize to the LGBTQ community for my insensitive words from my past.”

The Twitter posts from 2011 and earlier were scrutinized after news of Hart's role in the Academy Awards. “Yo if my son comes home & try’s 2 play with my daughters doll house I’m going 2 break it over his head & say n my voice ‘stop that’s gay,’” one since-deleted tweet said.

Benjamin Lee, an editor at The Guardian, shared some of Hart's tweets as screenshots. “Hart’s obsession,” Lee wrote in The Guardian, “with making unfunny, disrespectful, and inappropriate jokes about a community he has shown nothing but bile for along with a string of unrepentant responses to any criticism paints him as someone entirely undeserving of a spot on the Oscars stage.”

The hiring of Hart initially looked like a major win for the Oscars, which have seen their ratings and relevance sharply fall in recent years. Yes, the awards are still a huge platform watched by tens of millions of people. But everyone is tuning in to see if the host makes them laugh, and the host has to deal with a staid, overly-long format that makes that far from easy.
