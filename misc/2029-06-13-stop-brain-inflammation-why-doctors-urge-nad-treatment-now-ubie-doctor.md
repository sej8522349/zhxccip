Why Your Doctor Suggests NAD+ for Reducing Brain Inflammation

Neuro-inflammation is the immune response in your brain and spinal cord. While a certain level of inflammation helps protect you from injury or infection, chronic neuro-inflammation can contribute to memory issues, mood changes, and even neurodegenerative diseases. Increasingly, doctors are looking at cellular energy pathways—particularly the coenzyme NAD+—as a way to calm this harmful, chronic inflammation.

What Is NAD+?

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) is a vital coenzyme found in every cell. It plays a central role in:

As we age or face stressors (poor diet, lack of sleep, environmental toxins), NAD+ levels naturally decline. Lower NAD+ may impair cell repair, weaken mitochondria (the cell's "power plants"), and dysregulate immune responses—setting the stage for chronic neuro-inflammation.

How NAD+ and Neuro-Inflammation Are Connected

Energy and Mitochondrial Health

Mitochondria need NAD+ to produce ATP (cellular fuel).

When ATP falls, cells send distress signals that can trigger inflammatory pathways.

Sirtuin Activation

Sirtuins are enzymes that use NAD+ to control inflammation, stress resistance, and longevity.

Higher NAD+ boosts sirtuin activity, helping to turn down inflammatory signaling.

DNA Repair and PARPs

DNA damage is common with oxidative stress. PARP enzymes repair DNA but consume NAD+ in the process.

By restoring NAD+ levels, cells can repair damage without triggering excess inflammation.

Modulating Immune Cells

Microglia (brain's immune cells) activate during injury or infection.

Adequate NAD+ levels help microglia shift from a pro-inflammatory to a healing mode.

Clinical Evidence Supporting NAD+ for Brain Health

Research from peer-reviewed journals and clinical trials indicates:

Animal Studies: Rodents given NAD+ precursors show reduced markers of brain inflammation and improved cognitive performance.

Safety and Side Effects

Generally Well Tolerated: NR and NMN have shown good safety profiles in doses up to 1,000 mg per day in short-term studies.

Possible Mild Effects: Transient nausea, flushing, or headaches may occur.

Drug Interactions: Inform your doctor about all medications and supplements to avoid interactions, especially with chemotherapy agents or immunosuppressants.
