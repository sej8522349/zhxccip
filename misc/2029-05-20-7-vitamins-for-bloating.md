Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)

Vitamin B1 is an essential nutrient that plays a key role in the metabolism of carbohydrates. It helps convert food into energy, which can aid digestion and reduce bloating. Research has also shown that vitamin B1 may help improve gut health by increasing the production of beneficial gut bacteria.2

Good sources of vitamin B1 include pork, whole grains, and nuts.

Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

Vitamin B2 is another important nutrient that helps convert food into energy. It also plays a role in the metabolism of fats and proteins, which can aid digestion and reduce bloating. Research has also suggested that vitamin B2 may help relieve symptoms of irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), a condition that can cause bloating and other digestive issues.

Good sources of vitamin B2 include dairy products, eggs, and leafy green vegetables.

Vitamin B3 (Niacin)

Vitamin B3 is essential for energy production and helps support a healthy digestive system. It can also help reduce inflammation in the gut, which can contribute to bloating. Research has also suggested that vitamin B3 may help alleviate symptoms of IBS.

Good sources of vitamin B3 include poultry, fish, and whole grains.

Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)

Vitamin B5 is involved in the metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats, which can aid digestion and reduce bloating. It also helps support the production of enzymes that break down food in the digestive system. Research has also suggested that vitamin B5 may help reduce inflammation in the gut.3

Good sources of vitamin B5 include avocado, mushrooms, and whole grains.

Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)

Vitamin B6 plays a key role in the metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats, which can aid digestion and reduce bloating. It also helps regulate levels of the hormone serotonin, which can affect gut motility and reduce symptoms of bloating. Research has also suggested that vitamin B6 may help alleviate premenstrual bloating in women.

Good sources of vitamin B6 include poultry, fish, and bananas.

Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

Vitamin B12 is important for energy production and helps support a healthy digestive system. It also helps maintain the health of the nervous system, which can affect gut motility and reduce symptoms of bloating. Vitamin B12 may help reduce inflammation in the gut.

Good sources of vitamin B12 are fish, meat, and dairy products.

Vitamin D

Vitamin D is important for the absorption of calcium, which is essential for healthy bones. However, vitamin D may help reduce inflammation in the gut and improve digestive health.

Research suggests that individuals with low levels of vitamin D may experience gut microbiome imbalances, which can lead to bloating and other digestive issues.4

Good sources of vitamin D include fatty fish, mushrooms, and egg yolks.
