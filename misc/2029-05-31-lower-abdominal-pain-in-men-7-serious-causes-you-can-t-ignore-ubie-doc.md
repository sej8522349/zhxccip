What causes lower abdominal pain in males?

Lower abdominal pain in males has many possible causes, including:

Digestive issues: constipation, gas, gastroenteritis, IBS, or appendicitis

Urinary problems: UTIs, kidney stones, or bladder inflammation

Explanation

Lower abdominal pain in males is a common problem and can come from many different parts of the body. The lower abdomen contains organs from the digestive system, urinary tract, and reproductive system, as well as muscles, nerves, and blood vessels. Because so many structures are located in this area, the causes of abdominal pain can range from mild and temporary to serious and life-threatening.

This guide explains the most common reasons men experience lower abdominal pain, using clear and practical language based on established medical knowledge. It is not meant to replace medical care, but it can help you understand what may be happening and when to take action.

Lower abdominal pain is typically felt below the belly button and may occur on the right side, left side, or across the entire lower abdomen. The pain can feel:

Pain may also spread to the groin, lower back, or testicles. Paying attention to the location, intensity, and timing of abdominal pain can provide useful clues about the cause.

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

IBS is a long-term condition that affects how the intestines function. Abdominal pain is often linked to:

Stress

Certain foods

Changes in bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or both)

Appendicitis

Appendicitis typically starts with pain near the belly button that moves to the lower right abdomen. It often becomes severe and does not go away. This is a medical emergency and requires prompt treatment.

Urinary Tract Causes

Problems involving the urinary system are another common source of lower abdominal pain in males.

Kidney Stones

Bladder Issues

Inflammation or irritation of the bladder can cause discomfort or pressure in the lower abdomen, especially when the bladder is full.

Reproductive System Causes

The male reproductive organs can also contribute to lower abdominal pain.

Epididymitis

This condition involves inflammation of the epididymis, a tube at the back of the testicle. Pain may start in the groin or lower abdomen and spread to the testicle.

Testicular Torsion

Testicular torsion occurs when the spermatic cord twists, cutting off blood flow to the testicle. Pain is sudden, severe, and often accompanied by swelling. This is a medical emergency.

Prostatitis

Lower abdominal or pelvic pain

Muscles, Nerves, and Other Causes

Not all abdominal pain comes from internal organs.

Muscle Strain

Overuse or injury to the abdominal muscles can cause localized pain, especially with movement, coughing, or lifting.

Hernias

A hernia occurs when tissue pushes through a weak spot in the abdominal wall. It may cause a noticeable bulge and discomfort in the lower abdomen or groin.

Nerve Pain

Compressed or irritated nerves in the lower spine or pelvis can cause referred abdominal pain.

Sudden, severe, or worsening pain

Fever and chills

Persistent vomiting

Blood in urine or stool

Final Thoughts

Lower abdominal pain in males can come from many different causes, including digestive problems, urinary issues, reproductive conditions, and muscle or nerve-related concerns. Many causes are mild and resolve on their own, but some can be serious and require prompt medical care.

Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, if the pain is severe, or if symptoms are getting worse, speak to a doctor as soon as possible. Early evaluation can make a significant difference, especially when dealing with conditions that may be life-threatening or require urgent treatment.
