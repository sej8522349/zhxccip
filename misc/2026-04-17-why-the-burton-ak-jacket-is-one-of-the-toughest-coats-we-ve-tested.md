# Why the Burton AK Jacket Is One of the Toughest Coats We've Tested

THE EARNSLAW Burn Track in the South Island of New Zealand is a mighty 17-mile hike with the highlight being able to get right up close to the Earnslaw Glacier. Most people who hike this track spend a night in a tent near the Glacier. I wanted to get closer so I did what Tom Cruise would do—actually what he did do, as this is where he filmed those final epic scenes in Mission Impossible: Fallout—I took a helicopter direct to the top of the Glacier.

My coat of choice for such an adventure? A Burton AK Flare, one of the brand's toughest and most popular down jackets from the AK collection. According Burton, the "AK" stands for Alaska, the location where the products are first designed and tested. While the AK Flare is only offered in women's right now, Burton's AK collection expands into men's and women's offerings of jackets, pants, bibs, fleece, and more. (If you're looking for a men's jacket that's similar to the Flare, you'll want to check out the AK Hover.) The main takeaway from AK gear is it's built for extreme environments, especially when worn for snow sports and backcountry excursions.

With the AK Flare it all starts with a feathery 800-fill high-loft down that provides incredible warmth. In order to ensure an iron-clad waterproof and windproof seal, the AK Flare is outfitted with GORE-TEX and seam taped. These two specs were all I needed to hear when I was choosing a winter coat for this trip, as I wanted something that could not only keep me warm on the glacier but also keep me comfortable as I walked through intermittent high winds (helicopter landings included).

I boarded the helicopter in Queenstown, a twin engine beauty that travels at a smooth 125 mph. Within minutes we were traveling within what felt like mere inches away from the epic snowcapped mountain range as the pilot scouted for a spot to bring the bird down. The rotor of the helicopter continued to furiously spin, and I ducked low, Mission Impossible style, and started running out onto the glacier we just landed on. It was one of the biggest adrenaline shocks of my life. I wondered, is this where the saying “getting high” actually comes from?

Hiking across the top of the glacier I shored up my AK Flare's pit zips and tightened the wrist gaiters to shut out any snow that could be blow into my coat from the wind. When the gusts died down, I'd unzipped my front pocket of my jacket and pulled out my phone to take photos. I was pleased to see that none of the moisture from the snow was able to reach my phone, all thanks to the Flare's durable seam tape construction.
