Since running is an activity that depends on movement that takes place mostly in the sagittal plane (front-to-back movements), I suggested they incorporate more side-to-side (frontal plane) and rotational (transverse plane) exercises. One side-to-side exercise they both loved is the Cossack squat. I do it in the gym to build my glutes and quads with a load, and also as a stretch and warm up for my hips, hamstrings, and groin before running.

But as a short to middle distance runner for the last 30-plus years myself, I know firsthand how the ankles, knees and hips can make the movement tough to pull off at times. I often do an assisted version of the Cossack squat when my joints aren’t cooperating. All you need for this is something sturdy, like a yoga block, to help with your balance. But, with many clients I’ve used something even bigger, like a small stool or chair, to give more support to the movement.

To set up, place your stool about two feet directly in front of you. Stand with your legs wide apart and feet flat on the floor, pointing outward about 45 degrees. This is your starting position. Place your hands on the stool for support, then shift your weight to the right while bending your right knee, but keep your left leg fully extended. As you descend into the squat, allow your left foot to turn upward toward the ceiling. Lower yourself only as deep as your knees and hips will comfortably allow.

Bracing yourself with the stool will help you keep your chest up and lock in your core. At the bottom of the squat, you will feel a great stretch in the groin. After pausing at the bottom for a couple of seconds, it’s time to come back up. If you’ve gone down too far and don’t have the quad and glute power to propel your body upward, don’t be embarrassed to use the stool for leverage. I’ve used it plenty of times myself, and it’s better than falling back on your butt. When you come all the way back up, repeat the same steps for your left side.

If the assisted version of the Cossack squat is too easy for you, you can increase the challenge by removing the stool and/or using weights for more resistance. Most people use a dumbbell with a goblet hold, or a kettlebell with a front rack. But, before adding weights, it’s best to drill in the assisted version first. I suggest trying 3 sets of 5 to 8 repetitions on leg days.
