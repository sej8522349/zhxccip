What Are Peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids, which are the building blocks of proteins. They occur naturally in the body and play a crucial role in regulating a wide range of biological functions, including hormone production, immune response, and cell signaling.

Unlike larger proteins, peptides are small enough to be easily absorbed by the body and can be tailored to influence specific physiological processes. This makes them useful in treating various conditions such as inflammation, hormonal imbalances, aging-related issues, and even athletic performance and recovery.

Who Should Not Take Peptides?

Peptides may not be suitable for everyone. Individuals with a history of cancer, uncontrolled diabetes, severe heart or kidney disease, or active infections should avoid peptide therapy unless closely supervised by a medical professional. Pregnant or breastfeeding women should also not use peptides due to limited safety data. Additionally, those with hormone-sensitive conditions or autoimmune disorders should consult a healthcare provider before starting treatment. Proper evaluation and medical oversight are essential to ensure safety and avoid potential complications. Always seek professional advice before beginning any peptide therapy.
