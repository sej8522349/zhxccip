What Is a Pinched Nerve?

A pinched nerve (nerve compression) occurs when surrounding tissues—muscles, tendons, or bone—apply too much pressure on a nerve. In an office setting, this often happens in the neck, shoulder, wrist (carpal tunnel), or lower back.

Common signs include:

Sharp, burning or aching pain

Muscle weakness

Symptoms that worsen with certain positions or movements

While a pinched nerve usually isn't life-threatening, it can become chronic or cause lasting damage if ignored. If you experience sudden weakness, loss of coordination, or severe pain, always speak to a doctor right away.

How Red Light Therapy Works

Red light therapy—also called low-level laser therapy or photobiomodulation—uses specific wavelengths of red and near-infrared light to penetrate the skin and target cells. Key effects include:

Increased mitochondrial function: Light energy helps cells produce more ATP, the fuel for repair and regeneration.

Reduced inflammation: RLT can decrease pro-inflammatory markers, easing swelling around compressed nerves.

Improved blood flow: Enhanced circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients to damaged tissue.

Pain modulation: RLT may affect nerve signaling, lowering pain perception.

Because it's non-invasive and drug-free, RLT is appealing for in-office use. You can apply it right at your desk or in a break room with a small, portable device.

What the Research Says

Several clinical studies and reviews support RLT's role in nerve compression:

A 2017 review in the Journal of Biophotonics noted that photobiomodulation improved nerve regeneration and functional recovery in peripheral nerve injuries.

Research published in Lasers in Medical Science (2020) found that near-infrared RLT significantly reduced pain and improved grip strength in carpal tunnel syndrome patients.

Animal studies in Neuroscience Letters show accelerated nerve healing and reduced inflammation after low-level laser exposure.

While more large-scale human trials are needed, existing data suggest RLT can be a valuable part of a multi-modal strategy for office-related nerve compression.

When to See a Doctor

Most people can manage a mild pinched nerve with ergonomic tweaks, RLT and home care. However, schedule a medical evaluation if you experience:

Severe or worsening pain not relieved by conservative measures

Sudden muscle weakness or difficulty walking

Take-Home Points

Red light therapy for nerve compression and work is a non-invasive tool that may reduce pain and speed recovery.

Use a proper RLT device (600–900 nm wavelengths, 20–50 mW/cm²) for 5–10 minutes per session, once or twice daily.

Combine RLT with ergonomic adjustments, stretches and strengthening exercises for best results.

Monitor your progress, and if symptoms persist or worsen, speak to a doctor right away.

By integrating RLT into your office routine, you can address the effects of a pinched nerve without missing deadlines or sacrificing productivity. Always remember: if you have any concerns about serious symptoms, consult a healthcare professional.
